## Part 0. Protections that apply to every step

### Anti-retaliation (RPL § 223-b)

- A landlord may not serve a notice to quit, start an eviction, or substantially change the terms of the tenancy (including refusing to renew or offering a renewal with an unreasonable rent increase) in retaliation for: a good-faith complaint to the landlord or a government agency about a health or safety code violation, the warranty of habitability, or the duty to repair under MDL §§ 78, 79, 80; good-faith action to enforce the lease or those rights; or taking part in a tenants' organization [st-rpl-223-b (1), (2)].
- If the landlord acts within **one year** after the complaint, the action, or an inspection or order resulting from it, retaliation is presumed and the landlord must prove a non-retaliatory motive [st-rpl-223-b (5)].
- Retaliation is an affirmative defense to an eviction; the tenant still owes rent [st-rpl-223-b (4)]. The tenant can sue for damages, attorney's fees and an injunction [st-rpl-223-b (3)].
- A lease fee or penalty for filing a complaint is void, and a landlord who tries to collect one owes triple the amount [st-rpl-223-b (5-a)].
- **Coverage**: all rental residential premises except owner-occupied dwellings with fewer than four units; not where the tenant or household caused the condition [st-rpl-223-b (6)]. So a tenant renting in an owner-occupied two- or three-family house is **not** covered. Whether § 223-b applies to NYCHA: `unsettled` (the section doesn't mention public housing).

### Harassment as a related protection

Repeated failure to correct B or C violations within the correction period, repeated false certifications, interruptions of essential services, and changing the lock without giving a key are all listed as harassment under the HMC [nyc-hmc-27-2004 (a)(48)(b)-(b-3), (f)], which the owner must not commit [nyc-hmc-27-2005 (d)]. A court finding of harassment is a class C violation [nyc-hmc-27-2115 (m)(1)]. See path D.

### Immigration status

HPD tells tenants their immigration status does not matter when making a complaint [proc-hpd-code-guide-what-to-expect; proc-hpd-lead-based-paint].

**HP action details** (NYC Housing Court):
- What it is: a case asking the court to order the landlord to make repairs and correct violations, including lack of heat, hot water or other services; filed by one tenant or a group, for the apartment or public areas [proc-court-hp-starting; proc-np-hca-what-is-an-hp-action]. The Housing Part's jurisdiction is in [st-ccact-110].
- **Forms: the clerk hands them out.** "The clerk will give you forms to fill out called an 'Order to Show Cause Directing the Correction of Violations (HP Action),' and a 'Verified Petition in Support of an Order to Show Cause Directing the Correction of Violation.'" List every condition in each room and in the public areas [proc-court-hp-starting]. No copy of these two forms was collected (workstream 3 found they aren't online); Housing Court Answers also says to "get the forms and instructions to start an HP action from the clerk" [proc-np-hca-how-do-i-file-an-hp-action].
- Forms collected: Tenant's Request for Inspection (HPD Form A-B), which asks whether a child under six lives in the unit or spends more than 10 hours a week there [proc-court-form-hp-inspection-request]; fee waiver application UCS-FW1 [proc-court-form-ucs-fw1; proc-court-form-ucs-fw1-fillable] and NYC Civil Court fee waiver form [proc-court-form-civ-gp-15-fee-waiver]; instructions for serving the pro se HP Order to Show Cause and Verified Petition, CIV-LT-66 [proc-court-form-civ-lt-66]: the clerk keeps the originals; serve one set on the landlord in person (or by certified mail, return receipt requested, if the judge permits) and mail one set by certified mail, return receipt requested, to HPD Housing Litigation Bureau, 100 Gold Street, New York, NY 10038; then complete an Affidavit of Service (CIV-LT-25, **not collected**) with the receipts, signed before a notary or the clerk, and bring it to the hearing [proc-court-form-civ-lt-66]; an LT affirmation of service of an order to show cause, CIV-LT-19b, was also collected [proc-court-form-civ-lt-19b] (whether HP uses it rather than CIV-LT-25: `unsettled`); order to show cause to restore an HP case to the calendar for a compliance hearing and civil penalties when the landlord doesn't comply, CIV-LT-22 [proc-court-form-civ-lt-22]. Not usable in NYC: the RPAPL 7-D repair forms UCS-LT12A to LT12E and 7-C forms UCS-LT11A to LT11C [proc-court-form-ucs-lt12a; proc-court-form-ucs-lt11a] (an Article 7-D proceeding "shall not be maintained in any court within the counties of Nassau, Suffolk, Richmond, New York, Bronx, Kings or Queens" [st-rpapl-797 (3)]; Article 7-C "shall not apply to dwellings located in the city of New York" [st-rpapl-796-a (4)]).
- Steps: file in the county where the apartment is; bring the landlord's or managing agent's name and address; pay the fee by cash, certified check, money order or bank check, or apply to proceed as a poor person; the clerk notarizes the petition, assigns an inspection date if requested, and the HP judge signs the order to show cause; serve the landlord and HPD (and Corporation Counsel if proceeding as a poor person) as the order directs, often by certified mail with envelopes from the clerk; file an affirmation of service [proc-court-hp-starting]. The court may allow the tenant to serve by certified or registered mail [nyc-hmc-27-2115 (j)].
- Fee: `unsettled`. Housing Court Answers' 2017 information sheet says "It costs $45 to file the case" [proc-np-hca-infosheet-hp-2017] (secondary, 2017). The court's own fee page lists "Issue a notice of petition $45.00" but no HP-specific line [proc-court-fees].
- Emergencies: if the judge believes the condition is an emergency, the hearing is within about a week; tell the clerk it is urgent. An inspection may not happen before an emergency hearing [proc-court-hp-emergency-repairs; proc-court-hp-starting].
- Evidence: an inspection report isn't required; photographs and testimony can prove conditions [proc-court-hp-appearing-tenant].
- Outcome: the court directs the owner to correct and imposes penalties if not corrected [nyc-hmc-27-2115 (h)(1), (i)].

**Retaliation protection for path A**: every step is a protected act under RPL § 223-b: complaints to the landlord or to HPD, DHCR or the court about code violations or the warranty of habitability (1)(a), and good-faith actions such as an HP action or DHCR complaint (1)(b); the one-year presumption runs from the complaint, the action, or the resulting inspection or order (5) [st-rpl-223-b]. Joining with neighbours (group HP action, 7-A) is also protected as tenants' organization activity (1)(c) [st-rpl-223-b].

**Retaliation protection for path C**: a DHCR complaint, a warranty-of-habitability defense or counterclaim, and a 7-A petition are good-faith actions to enforce rights under the warranty of habitability or housing laws [st-rpl-223-b (1)(b), (5)(b)]. Note that a retaliation defense does not relieve the tenant of rent otherwise owed [st-rpl-223-b (4)], and in a rent-impairing defense the tenant must still deposit the rent with the court clerk [st-mdl-302-a (3)(c)].
