How we tested Tenard

We ran Tenard and a single model call on the same photos and videos, none of which we prepared for. Here is what happened, including where it fails.

The test

175 photos and 22 videos from public, licensed sources, chosen by a seeded script. One of us checked every label by hand. The labels and scoring rules were frozen with checksums at 08:03 on November 21, before any app code existed. More than half the photos show no problem, or something that only looks like one.

The baseline is one call to Gemini 4 Argon with the photo or the whole video, asked what violations it shows and to cite the law, class and deadline. Tenard has a model propose problems, the Decisions API confirm each one, and code look up the class and deadline.

Photos

Overall accuracy is a tie. Tenard makes fewer wrong reports and fewer false alarms on look-alikes. The baseline finds more real problems.

Video

Tenard checks a still about every three seconds and reports a problem only if two or more agree. It raised no false alarms, and it missed problems that were on screen only briefly.

Mould size

Size sets the violation class: under 10 square feet is Class A, 10 to under 30 is B, 30 or more is C. Compared with our reviewer's judgement:

The baseline agreed with the reviewer more often. Tenard declines when it can't tell and asks for the size or a clearer photo. Neither handles the middle size well.

What we changed after seeing failures

    Where it still fails

    • Leaks. Water stains get reported alongside the real problem more often than the baseline does it.
    • Mid-sized mould. Of six photos our reviewer put at 10 to under 30 square feet, Tenard got none right: two called smaller, four declined.
    • Missed problems. The check that removes false alarms also removes some real ones, mostly detectors and leaks.
    • Short moments in video. A problem visible for a few seconds can fall between the stills.
    • No predicted class for leaks or wall damage. HPD issues them in more than one class and the rule isn't published, so Tenard says the inspector decides.

    The test photos are public images, not NYC tenants' own, and these are small sets: a difference of a few items is noise. The decision rules are a draft that nobody with legal training has reviewed.
